# Course Story Structure

Use this reference when creating, adapting, or analyzing Yuhong training decks, workshop decks, co-creation meeting decks, or instructor-led courseware.

Primary source: `assets/reference-decks/trader-to-service-provider-course.pptx`.

This is a two-day, three-night courseware pattern for helping dealers move from trader logic to service-provider logic. It is not a normal report deck. It combines instructor pages, learner interaction, case proof, worksheet output, and operational tools.

## Core Course Thesis

The course thesis is:

Dealer growth must shift from selling products to operating service. The owner needs to rebuild mindset, choose a focused battlefield, design channel tactics, build a minimum team, and control delivery risk.

In Chinese course language:

- 从贸易商转型服务商.
- 从卖货思维转向用户思维.
- 从产品差价转向服务价值.
- 从短期成交转向长期口碑.
- 从老板单点驱动转向团队和流程承接.

## Full Narrative Arc

### 1. Opening And Alignment

Approximate source range: slides 1-10.

Job:

- Open the camp.
- Explain why the course exists.
- Build urgency around market change.
- Make "service" the answer to the business environment.

Reusable pattern:

1. State the training context.
2. Tell learners what they will gain: cases, experience, methods, tools.
3. Define the outside change: market, customer, competition.
4. Land one unavoidable conclusion.

Best used when:

- The audience still treats the topic as optional.
- The course needs to first create pressure and relevance.

Do not use when:

- Learners already accept the topic and only need tools.

### 2. Mindset Transformation

Approximate source range: slides 11-34.

Job:

- Change the learner's mental model before teaching tactics.
- Contrast trader thinking and service-provider thinking.
- Prepare learners for a "hard first, sweet later" growth curve.

Core contrasts:

- 卖货思维 vs 用户思维.
- 做贸易 vs 做品牌.
- 省成本 vs 投未来.
- 短期主义 vs 长期主义.

Reusable pattern:

1. Ask an interactive question.
2. Show a strong contrast.
3. Use a dealer quote or case line.
4. Convert the contrast into a principle.
5. Summarize in a comparison table.

Best used when:

- The audience is likely to resist investment, standardization, or team-building.
- The desired behavior requires a new identity, not just a new action.

Risk:

- If too long, this section becomes motivational preaching. Keep the contrast tied to concrete business consequences.

### 3. Growth Path And Stage Map

Approximate source range: slides 34-42.

Job:

- Show that transformation is staged.
- Reduce anxiety by naming the 0-1, 1.0, 2.0, and 3.0 path.
- Connect order volume, channel strategy, category strategy, and team system.

Reusable pattern:

1. Show the full growth curve.
2. Mark current stage.
3. Name the stage task.
4. Define the next milestone.

Best used when:

- Learners need to know "where am I now" and "what should I do next".

Risk:

- Do not imply all dealers must follow the same speed or same channel order.

### 4. Strategic Diagnosis: Three Looks

Approximate source range: slides 43-103.

Job:

- Help learners choose a battlefield.
- Move from general enthusiasm to local-market judgment.

Three looks:

- 看大势: user habits, channel trend, channel value.
- 看竞争: opponent type, opponent advantage, response strategy.
- 看自我: relationship, prior experience, team capability.

Output:

- Local market opportunity assessment.
- Core competition strategy.
- Channel resource assessment.
- Strategic positioning.
- Core battle map.

Reusable pattern:

1. Teach a simple lens.
2. Ask learners to rate their own market.
3. Fill a worksheet immediately.
4. Share and comment.
5. Convert the worksheet into one strategic decision.

Best used when:

- The course must help each dealer choose a different path based on local conditions.

Risk:

- Do not skip worksheet output. Without output, this becomes a lecture on strategy rather than a workshop.

### 5. Tactical Modules: Four Order Sources

Approximate source range: slides 104-179.

Job:

- Translate strategy into order-acquisition tactics.
- Teach four reusable business modes.

Four modes:

- 分销模式.
- 家装公司模式.
- 工长模式.
- C 端模式.

Common teaching structure:

1. Co-create customer/channel characteristics.
2. Explain the operating logic.
3. Give a step-by-step tactic.
4. Show a case or example.
5. Let learners complete a worksheet.

Best used when:

- The training objective is practical execution.
- The learners need to leave with channel action plans.

Risk:

- Do not teach all four modes with equal weight if the course is short. Select the modes according to learner stage and local opportunity.

### 6. Team Building

Approximate source range: slides 180-206.

Job:

- Make the service-provider model operationally feasible.
- Define minimum viable team, role split, staffing trigger, and incentive.

Core sequence:

1. Name the fear: building a team feels heavy.
2. Break service into key tasks.
3. Group tasks into functions.
4. Assign functions to current or future roles.
5. Define when to add dedicated staff.
6. Design incentive and growth path.

Best used when:

- Dealers understand the opportunity but worry they cannot execute.

Risk:

- Do not prescribe a large team at 0-1 stage. The source pattern emphasizes minimum practical configuration.

### 7. Risk Control And Closing

Approximate source range: slides 207-216.

Job:

- Prevent common delivery and financial risks.
- Close with disciplined action energy.

Core risks:

- Delivery boundary unclear.
- Payment and receivable risk.
- Third-party payment ambiguity.
- Process control missing.

Reusable pattern:

1. Ask learners what risks they have seen.
2. Name the few fatal risks.
3. Provide standard clauses, process, or tool.
4. Tie risk control back to service credibility.

Best used when:

- The course asks dealers to start real service delivery after class.

Risk:

- Avoid legal overclaiming. Present contract language as operational example unless it has been legally reviewed.

## Course Page Types

Use these page types when building a new courseware deck:

- Section opener: announces the module and emotional job.
- Instructor guide page: time, teaching objective, key points, execution notes.
- Concept contrast: two mental models or two practices.
- Principle page: one memorable sentence plus evidence.
- Case proof page: dealer quote, action, result, lesson.
- Co-creation question: one question, enough whitespace for discussion.
- Worksheet page: table learners can fill during class.
- Method model: formula, framework, stage map, or flow.
- Example page: completed sample to reduce learner ambiguity.
- Action commitment page: owner, target, timing, metric.

## Instructor-Led Rhythm

A strong module should follow this rhythm:

1. Why this matters.
2. What good looks like.
3. How to judge your own situation.
4. How to act.
5. What to fill in.
6. Who shares.
7. What the instructor comments on.

For a live workshop, do not stack more than 15-20 minutes of pure lecture without an interaction, worksheet, or case.

## Output Rules For New Courseware

- Keep the course outcome visible: what learner artifact will be produced.
- Every long concept section needs a worksheet or action output.
- Use real dealer language and field language.
- Keep instructor notes separate from audience-facing slide text.
- For each module, specify timing, teaching objective, key points, and execution notes.
- Do not copy the source deck visually unless the user explicitly asks for visual adaptation.
- Do not reuse the source's claims as current facts unless the source deck is cited or the claim is revalidated.
