# Quality Check

Use this reference before building or delivering an Oriental Yuhong dealer PPT. The goal is to prevent good-looking slides from replacing business judgment.

## Build Gate

Do not build the PPT until the outline passes these checks:

1. Every slide answers one business question.
2. Every major claim has data, case evidence, or a clearly labeled assumption.
3. Visual components are selected for business meaning, not coolness.
4. A dealer owner can understand the slide's main point within 10 seconds.
5. Next-month actions have owner/responsible role, target object, action, timing, and inspection metric.
6. Phenomena, causes, judgments, and actions are not mixed up.

If any check fails, revise the outline first.

## Slide-Level Checklist

For each slide, verify:

- Business question: What question does this slide answer?
- Main claim: What should the owner believe after seeing it?
- Evidence: Which metric, table, customer/SKU, case, or action record supports it?
- Implication: What does this mean for management?
- Next step: Does this slide create or support an action?
- Fit: Is this the simplest layout that can carry the message?

If a slide only displays data without implication, rewrite it.

## Evidence Rules

Use evidence in this order:

1. Current raw data.
2. Prior month or historical baseline.
3. Target vs actual.
4. Customer/SKU/channel detail.
5. Visit/action execution records.
6. Reference case or method deck.
7. Clearly labeled assumption.

Never present an assumption as a fact.

Use these labels when needed:

- Data-supported
- Directional
- Hypothesis
- Needs validation
- Not calculable from current data

## Component Overuse Check

Before using a cherry-red logic component, ask:

- Does it make the business point faster than a table/chart?
- Does the component structure match the metric logic?
- Will the owner understand it without explanation?
- Does the component force the data into a fake relationship?
- Is the slide more about visual impact than management insight?

Replace the component with a simpler table, chart, or action board when the answer is weak.

## 10-Second Owner Test

A slide passes when an owner can understand in 10 seconds:

- What happened.
- Whether it is good or bad.
- Why it matters.
- What should be inspected or done next.

Common failures:

- Title is only a topic label.
- Too many numbers compete for attention.
- Red decoration overwhelms the conclusion.
- The slide has a diagram but no decision.
- The action is a slogan rather than a management behavior.

## Phenomenon vs Cause Guardrail

Do not mistake phenomena for causes.

Phenomenon examples:
- Sales grew 30%.
- Active outlets declined.
- Home-decoration sales share rose.
- Member output is low.
- A few key customers contributed most growth.

Cause examples:
- Growth came from township outlet activation.
- Active outlets declined because visits concentrated on top accounts and small outlets were not maintained.
- Home-decoration sales rose because cooperating companies increased and order frequency improved.
- Member output is low because registered members are not being activated through meetings and repeat-purchase actions.

If the cause is not proven, write:

- "The data shows the phenomenon; the cause needs validation."
- "Likely driver, pending customer/SKU/visit detail."

## Action Quality Check

Every next-month action should include:

- Responsible role: boss, sales manager, channel owner, retail owner, home-decoration owner, finance, warehouse, etc.
- Target object: which customers, outlets, SKUs, members, home-decoration companies, townships, or channels.
- Action behavior: visit, activate, develop, review, push SKU, collect payment, hold meeting, set credit limit, route planning.
- Timing: daily, weekly, monthly, before a specific meeting, or by a date.
- Inspection metric: sales, active outlet count, order count, SKU penetration, collection, meeting count, visit completion, conversion rate.

Weak action:
- "Continue to deepen channels."

Better action:
- "Channel owner screens 20 low-active township outlets by Friday, completes two route visits per week, and reviews purchase conversion and SKU entry every Monday."

## Monthly Review Final Check

Before building, the outline should include:

- Core conclusion.
- Target achievement.
- Growth source or gap source.
- Structure diagnosis.
- One priority deep dive.
- Risks that change next-month management.
- 3-5 inspection-ready actions.

Optional but useful:

- Customer/SKU appendix.
- Metric definitions or calculation notes.
- Case analogy when it clarifies the action.

## Case Sharing Final Check

Before building a case deck, confirm:

- The opening thesis has tension.
- The background explains why this dealer's path matters.
- The timeline shows business changes, not just years.
- The action pages follow Before -> Action -> Now -> Management meaning.
- Risk lessons are not hidden.
- The ending says what other dealers can copy.

## Red Flags

Stop and revise if:

- More than one-third of slide titles are topic labels.
- A slide has no data, case evidence, or action implication.
- The outline uses "growth", "deepening", "empowerment", "quality", or "transformation" without concrete evidence.
- A visual component is selected before the business question is clear.
- The next-month plan has actions but no owner or metric.
- The deck praises performance but does not explain repeatability or risk.
